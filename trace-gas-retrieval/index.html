About the three parameters

A spectrometer measures sunlight after atmospheric absorption and reflection from the surface. The inverse problem estimates a parameter vector θ = (q, m, A). This example evaluates the posterior directly from a simple absorption model; every colored point is a candidate parameter vector.

q · Methane column scale
Vertical methane abundance relative to a chosen 1900 ppb reference: XCH₄ = 1900q ppb. A fixed dry-air column is assumed.
m · Effective light-path factor
Multiplier on a reference photon path. It represents uncertainty in absorption path length, not aerosol optical depth or a retrieved viewing angle.
A · Surface albedo
Fraction of light reflected by a Lambertian surface, assumed constant across both schematic bands.
Color · Relative posterior density
Purple through green to yellow indicates increasing density. All parameter axes are linear; the color mapping is logarithmic.

A small radiative-transfer inverse problem

For a reflecting surface under an absorbing atmosphere, normalized reflected radiance is the surface albedo multiplied by the two-way transmission. We combine the known solar and viewing geometry with the reference gas optical depths, and write:

Rⱼ(q, m, A) = A exp[−m(q kCH₄,ⱼ + kCO₂,ⱼ)]
yⱼ = Rⱼ(1.20, 1.05, 0.32) + εⱼ
J(θ) = Σⱼ∈used [Rⱼ(θ) − yⱼ]² / (2σ²)
p(θ | y) ∝ exp[−J(θ)] inside the prior bounds

R is πL divided by incident solar irradiance and the cosine of solar zenith angle. The coefficients k are dimensionless reference optical depths: gas cross section × reference column × reference air mass factor. CH₄ channels have kCO₂ = 0; CO₂ channels have kCH₄ = 0. The CO₂ column is treated as known.

All observations and optical-depth coefficients on this page are synthetic. They illustrate absorption and identifiability. Channel numbers are schematic spectral samples, not calibrated wavelengths or HITRAN lines. The chosen methane reference of 1900 ppb is a scaling convention.

The CH₄-only spectrum is exactly unchanged by q → cq and m → m/c within the bounds. More CH₄ channels constrain their product, but cannot separate its factors in this model. CO₂ absorption responds to m independently of q, which breaks this ambiguity.

A ridge is a continuous family of solutions. This model does not require separated modes. Its non-Gaussian shape comes from the exponential forward model and the product q × m.

References and connection to operational retrievals

Jongaramrungruang et al. (2021), Remote sensing of methane plumes, AMT 14, 7999–8017: equations 1–2 supply the reflected-sunlight and Beer–Lambert structure used here. That study includes realistic spectra, surface reflectance and instrument effects; this page reduces the physics to a few idealized channels.

Chan Miller et al. (2024), Methane retrieval from MethaneAIR using the CO₂ proxy approach, AMT 17, 5429–5454: motivates using CO₂ to constrain the common absorption path. This toy jointly fits a single effective path factor; it is a pedagogical analogue of the proxy principle, not a reproduction of their retrieval algorithm.

The simplifications are a fixed atmospheric state, a known CO₂ column, a common wavelength-independent path factor, and one grey albedo for both bands. There is no explicit multiple scattering, vertical profile, interfering water vapor, instrument convolution, wavelength shift, or uncertain CO₂. Real instruments need these effects treated or screened. In particular, scattering does not generally reduce to one scalar path factor.

Prior, uncertainty, and numerical method

Independent uniform priors: 0.5 ≤ q ≤ 2.5; 0.5 ≤ m ≤ 1.7; 0.16 ≤ A ≤ 0.48. Density is with respect to these linear coordinates. Likelihood errors are independent Gaussian with the selected σ. The fixed synthetic noise realization was generated with standard deviation 0.006 (seed 42); changing σ changes the assumed uncertainty only.

A regular 81 × 81 × 81 grid evaluates the 3D posterior. Dots are grid locations, not Monte Carlo samples. The marginal surface integrates out albedo using 121 midpoint nodes and rescales to its own maximum. The uncertainty table uses tensor-product trapezoidal weights over the full 3D grid, including regions below the display threshold; central intervals are approximate grid quantiles.

At each (q, m), albedo is linear in radiance, so its best fit is solved analytically and clipped to the bounds. An 81 × 81 search followed by local refinement finds a representative best fit. With CH₄ alone, equally good fits exist along the ridge or sheet. A best-fit marker represents one of them.

The cut uses normalized coordinates u = ((q−1.5)/1, (m−1.1)/0.6, (A−0.32)/0.16) and n·u = d. Its flat map evaluates density on that plane and uses the 3D maximum; it does not integrate over other parameters. Color is log relative density, and 100% means the peak value rather than 100% probability. A spinning cut can miss the high-density region; “Align cut to current view” passes it through the selected parameters.

No MCMC, Hessian approximation, or sampling benchmark is used on this page. The forward model and data are embedded, so this file runs offline after downloading.
