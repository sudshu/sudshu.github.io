🛰️ Scientist at **NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory**.
Methane Modeling Lead for the **U.S. Greenhouse Gas Center**.
I quantify greenhouse-gas emissions from space — from single leaking wells
to the global carbon cycle.

## About

My research stitches satellite remote sensing to atmospheric modeling. A few
threads I keep returning to:

- **Catching methane leaks from orbit.** Our
  [2019 PNAS paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1908712116) was the
  first satellite detection of an unreported extreme methane leak — a
  natural-gas well blowout that turned out to be one of the largest U.S.
  point-source emissions on record. It helped kick off a wave of operational
  satellite-based plume monitoring.
- **Taking Earth's carbon pulse from space.** I developed satellite-derived
  whole-atmosphere CO₂ growth-rate methods now part of the Global Carbon
  Project workflow
  ([AGU Adv. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023AV001145);
  [Nat. Commun. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-61588-2);
  [AGU Adv. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025AV002085)).
- **AI for atmospheric science.** Building CNNs for emission-source detection
  and quantification, ML emulators for atmospheric tracer transport, and
  LLM-based agentic systems to automate and accelerate climate and
  atmospheric research.

I did my **PhD in physics at Utrecht University** (2017) and an integrated
BS-MS in Earth Sciences at IISER Kolkata. Before JPL I spent six years at
SRON, the Netherlands Institute for Space Research.

## What I'm working on now

- ML/physics hybrids for global methane and CO₂ inverse modeling.
- Multi-instrument satellite methane plume detection (Sentinel-2 / 3 / 5P,
  PRISMA, EMIT and friends).
- LLM-based autonomous research agents for atmospheric inversions and
  scientific discovery.
- Side projects — like the [waterfall ML benchmark](/2026/06/08/yosemite-waterfall-ml-vs-4dvar.html) below.
